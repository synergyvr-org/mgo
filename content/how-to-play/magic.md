Vanilla magic was arguably not as disappointing as vanilla melee combat, but it left something to be desired. You point your hand, you hold a trigger, fire comes out. It works, but for a game that lets you _be a freaking wizard in VR_, it doesn't quite deliver on the fantasy. Naturally, modders couldn't leave it alone. Mad God's Overhaul leans on a pile of mods to make spellcasting feel more like... well, _magic_.

## Selecting spells

In vanilla, swapping what's equipped means opening menus, which is a real momentum killer. {{< nexus 47630 >}}Spell Wheel VR{{< /nexus >}} fixes that with a radial menu you can open with a button combo, letting you swap spells (and shouts, and items) on the fly. You can configure the wheel to show only favorites, only spells from certain schools, etc. It has a ton of options, and it's for more than just spells. For the full tour, see the [Spell Wheel VR](/mod-highlights/spell-wheel) page.

## Casting

Several mods work together to make the actual _casting_ feel better in VR.

{{< nexus 55751 >}}Magic Improvements for Skyrim VR{{< /nexus >}} makes casting look better in several ways, including better dual-casting, improved aiming, and particle effects that scale according to your remaining magicka. {{< nexus 92804 >}}Dual Casting Fix VR{{< /nexus >}} fixes a bug that made dual-casting behave like single-casting in VR.

{{< nexus 119689 >}}Spell Auto-Aim VR{{< /nexus >}} gives your projectiles a little help finding their mark. It ships tuned to help just a _little_, and it's configurable with an [INI](/reference/editing-inis) if you want more (or less) assistance.

{{< nexus 164902 >}}SpellBender VR{{< /nexus >}}, one of Machina's many mods in the list, lets you steer magical projectiles. It's very cool, and it's configurable with an [INI](/reference/editing-inis). Check out the {{< nexus 164902 >}}Nexus page{{< /nexus >}} for details.

## Weapon infusions

{{< nexus 58023 >}}Conduit - Short-term Weapon Infusions{{< /nexus >}} lets you temporarily infuse your weapon with the effect of a spell you have equipped in your other hand. It combines with existing enchantments, and it even works on bound weapons. Conducted effects work with [Weapon Throw VR](/mod-highlights/weapon-throw) too! It's very, very cool.

## In with the new...

{{< nexus 27839 >}}Mysticism - A Magic Overhaul{{< /nexus >}} rebalances Skyrim's magic system and adds new spells across every school (while bringing back some fan-favorite Elder Scrolls spells of yore). {{< nexus 95196 >}}Sorcerer{{< /nexus >}} does the same for staves and scrolls.

On top of that foundation sits a set of themed spell packs, gathered in the list's **Spell Extensions** section. Most are built to stay balanced against vanilla spells.

* {{< nexus 69938 >}}Astral Magic 2{{< /nexus >}} draws on Aetherius to smite people with the power of the stars, and {{< nexus 80852 >}}Lunaris{{< /nexus >}} does moon magic.
* Ride the lightning[^1] with {{< nexus 89643 >}}Storm Calling Magic 2{{< /nexus >}} and {{< nexus 90405 >}}Arclight{{< /nexus >}}.
* {{< nexus 77826 >}}Natura{{< /nexus >}} handles nature, {{< nexus 84304 >}}Bloodmoon{{< /nexus >}} handles blood, and {{< nexus 90832 >}}Desecration{{< /nexus >}} handles the necrotic stuff (including a chance to spawn a flaming necrotic familiar 🔥🤘🔥.
* {{< nexus 97892 >}}Abyssal Tides Magic{{< /nexus >}} is your one-stop shop for "forbidden knowledge and dark powers."

{{< nexus 124125 >}}Wizarding Traversal Magic{{< /nexus >}} gives you magical transportation. **Apparition** teleports you to where you're aiming, **Apparition Travel** is a toggle that lets you fast travel instantly in in-game time, and **Mist Apparition** turns you into an invulnerable mist to evade attacks[^2], with a bonus to speed and jump height. Note that these are _powers_, so they compete with shouts for your Voice slot. You can buy the tomes once you reach Alteration skill level 75.

New in 4.0 RC3, {{< nexus 35464 >}}A Guiding Light{{< /nexus >}} replaces Clairvoyance with **Guiding Wisp**, which summons a wisp that leads you to your nearest quest objective. You'll find the tome in Embershard Mine, or you can buy it from the Riverwood Trader.

{{< aside type="btw" title="Quality over quantity" >}}
RC3 pared down the collection of spell packs considerably, favoring a smaller, curated set. A few names you may remember (Arcane, Inquisition, Necrom, and others) are no longer in the list.
{{< /aside >}}

## Magic enemies

The spell packs aren't just for you. A set of {{< nexus 133212 >}}NPC Spell Variance{{< /nexus >}} patches hands the expanded spell pool to Skyrim's own spellcasters, so the mages, necromancers, and vampires you run into draw from the same library you do.

Balance cuts the other way too. {{< nexus 107812 >}}NPC Mage Balance FIX{{< /nexus >}} takes away the unfair advantages enemy mages enjoy in vanilla, where they get to play by friendlier rules than you do for magicka cost and regeneration.

{{< aside type="btw" title="Fair warning" >}}
An enemy mage with a bigger spell list is an enemy mage with more ways to ruin your Sundas. Ward early and often.
{{< /aside >}}

## Sights and sounds

Magic in MGO doesn't just behave differently. It looks and sounds different too.

Vanilla's hand effects are 2D sprites animating in a single flat plane, which is an adequate illusion on a monitor, but pretty obvious in VR. {{< nexus 50197 >}}3D Casting FX{{< /nexus >}} and {{< nexus 92565 >}}3D Casting Shock{{< /nexus >}} rebuild them as actual 3D effects.

{{< nexus 29817 >}}Arctic - Frost Effects Redux{{< /nexus >}} brings new 3D meshes and new textures to frost spells, and a stack of VFX edits touch up lightning, healing, light, drain, and summoning portals. {{< nexus 27456 >}}FleshFX{{< /nexus >}} spruces up armor spells (Oakflesh and friends), and {{< nexus 3532 >}}Frozen Electrocuted Combustion{{< /nexus >}} means the things you kill with magic appear appropriately burned, frozen, or electrified.

{{< nexus 12939 >}}Deadly Spell Impacts{{< /nexus >}} leaves scorch marks and craters where your spells land, and {{< nexus 67845 >}}Spell Rune Retexture{{< /nexus >}} gives runes a glow-up (sorry).

{{< nexus 70400 >}}Master Spells Runes{{< /nexus >}} adds a flourish to master-level spells, borrowing the rune artwork from Strange Runes.

{{< nexus 145018 >}}V.A.E.R. - Visual Animated Enchants Reborn{{< /nexus >}} gives enchanted weapons animated shaders, with unique effects for unique weapons and artifacts. Its effects are dialed back by 60% compared to the original mod specifically to suit a first-person view. It also shows on _NPC_ weapons, so you get a moment's warning about what's about to be swung at you. (Staves aren't covered.) {{< nexus 105492 >}}Enchantment Art Extender{{< /nexus >}} distributes new enchantment effects based on config files.

{{< nexus 51541 >}}Airgetlam - Modern Magic Sounds Rework{{< /nexus >}} rebuilds Skyrim's spell audio, and {{< nexus 90696 >}}Airgetlam - Shouts SFX{{< /nexus >}} does the same for your Thu'um.

## ...Out with the old

Your spell list can get unwieldy pretty quickly. {{< nexus 51125 >}}Forget Spell{{< /nexus >}} and {{< nexus 15558 >}}Spell Organizer{{< /nexus >}} let you hide or remove spells you'll never cast again (or for the first time) so they stop cluttering up your menu.

## Optional magic mods

Two magic-related picks live in the **OPTIONAL Mods → Combat & Magic** folder (see [Onboarding](/start/onboarding#combat--magic)), disabled by default.

{{< nexus 19456 >}}Strange Runes{{< /nexus >}} gives rune spells showier visuals. If you enable it and want a different look, {{< nexus 137611 >}}Alternative Runes{{< /nexus >}} is a texture replacer for it (to be used alongside Strange Runes, not in place of it).

{{< aside type="btw" title="Spellsiphon" >}}
For a more radical overhaul, {{< nexus 26627 >}}Spellsiphon - Immersive Combat{{< /nexus >}} (also in **OPTIONAL Mods → Combat & Magic**, also off by default) turns magic into a fast, resource-juggling combat system with its own controls. It's a big departure from vanilla, but people who love it _really_ love it. It's designed to be learned in-game, so don't bother reading too much about it ahead of time. If you don't like it, it's safe to turn off mid-game.
{{< /aside >}}
