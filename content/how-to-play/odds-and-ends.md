The other pages in this chapter cover Skyrim's big systems: getting around, hitting things, casting spells. This one is the junk drawer. It's where a handful of mods live that make Tamriel feel more lived-in without fitting neatly under any of those headings. None of it is essential reading, but each of these is a small delight.

## Take a load off

{{< nexus 16613 >}}Be Seated{{< /nexus >}}, by CylonSurfer, syncs sitting down in the game with sitting down in real life. Plop into a chair, stool, or bench in Skyrim and the mod (after a quick fade to black and a {{< control name="trigger" >}} press to confirm) drops you into seated play, lined up with the actual seat under you. It's a treat for long sessions.

It goes well past furniture, though:

* **Order food and drink** in any tavern. Wave over a server, ask for a drink, and watch them pour it at the bar and carry it to your table, where you down it by hand. There's a full meal-ordering service too, eaten bite by bite.
* **Sit anywhere** with a button combo, cross-legged on a cliff's edge or up in the rafters of the Bannered Mare, wherever the view is worth it.
* **Sleep in a bed** you've rented by raising your hand to your mouth for a yawn.

{{< aside type="btw" title="Not sold on the prompts?" >}}
If the sit-down confirmation prompts aren't for you, you can switch them off in the MCM and let furniture behave the vanilla way while keeping the tavern service, sit-anywhere, and sleeping features.
{{< /aside >}}

## A world that reacts

Some of MGO's most satisfying little moments come from {{< nexus 149484 >}}Object Impact Framework{{< /nexus >}} (OIF), a plugin that lets objects respond when you grab, hit, throw, or set fire to them. On its own it's just plumbing. The fun comes from the config mods built on top of it, several of which are in the list:

* Playing as a brute who can't be bothered to develop the fine motor skills necessary to pick locks? Flimsy little locks still shouldn't stand between you and that sweet, sweet loot! {{< nexus 154901 >}}Break The Lock{{< /nexus >}} lets you smash your way through anything that's locked.
* {{< nexus 151000 >}}Burn Those Books{{< /nexus >}} lets you set fire to Skyrim's collective knowledge. You monster.
* Can't be bothered to hunt down a cooking pot? {{< nexus 153449 >}}Cooking with Fire{{< /nexus >}} lets you throw food on the ground and hit it with a fire spell to transform it into the cooked version.
* {{< nexus 150895 >}}Flammable Alcohol{{< /nexus >}} lets you channel your inner Molotov, sort of. Use a fire spell or fire-enchanted weapon on an alcoholic beverage, and watch it explode.
* {{< nexus 155775 >}}Melt The Ice{{< /nexus >}} lets you shrink icefloes with fire.

There are a few more in the same spirit, and together they turn a lot of Skyrim's set dressing into things you can actually mess with. If you like this sort of hands-on chaos, {{< nexus 128285 >}}More Interactive Objects - Throwing Things{{< /nexus >}} is worth a look too, letting you pick up and hurl clutter that used to be bolted to the table.

## A few more

A grab-bag of smaller tweaks worth knowing you've got:

* {{< nexus 63889 >}}Enhanced Invisibility VR{{< /nexus >}} fixes invisibility so everyday actions (opening doors, looting, harvesting, chatting) don't shatter the spell. Only going on the offensive breaks it, the way it always should have worked.
* {{< nexus 59512 >}}Enhanced Reanimation VR{{< /nexus >}} lets your reanimated thralls keep their equipment, spells, and skills, so raising a fallen bandit chief gets you a genuinely capable minion instead of a fragile husk.
* {{< nexus 62372 >}}Wash That Blood Off 2 VR{{< /nexus >}} lets you clean the caked-on blood off yourself after a messy fight, for when you'd rather not stroll into Whiterun looking like you lost one.
