{{< nexus 55653 >}}Nether's Follower Framework{{< /nexus >}} (NFF) turns "a follower" into a party you can actually run. Vanilla lets you drag a single companion around with little more than "follow me," "wait here," and a cramped trade menu. NFF hands you real command over your companions, and lets you travel with several of them at once.

## What it does

The short tour:

* **A real party.** Recruit several followers at once and keep tabs on the whole roster.
* **Commands that stick.** Set where each follower stands (at your side, at range, sneaking), how aggressively they fight, and when to wait, relax and sandbox, or head home, one at a time or all together.
* **Gear and outfits.** Manage a follower's inventory and lock in an outfit, so they wear what you handed them instead of reverting to their defaults.
* **Mounts and travel.** Followers can ride horses and keep pace on the road.
* **Tidy dismissal.** Send a follower home rather than losing track of where they wandered off to.

You reach most of this through NFF's own menu and the extra dialogue it adds to each follower, with the defaults set in its [MCM](/reference/glossary#mcm).

## Which followers to manage

NFF is built for the ordinary follower: a hireling, a random townsperson you've talked into adventuring, a vanilla companion. Point it at those freely.

{{< aside type="alert" title="Don't hand NFF a scripted follower" >}}
NFF manages a follower by taking over their AI and behavior. That's exactly right for a plain hireling, but exactly wrong for a companion who runs on their own custom logic: bespoke followers often ship with hand-written routines, banter, and combat AI that NFF will trample. Don't add a modded follower to the framework unless that follower's Nexus page specifically says it's NFF-compatible.
{{< /aside >}}

Because you decide which followers to bring under NFF's control, this is easy to honor: leave a custom companion to their own devices unless their author has blessed the pairing.
